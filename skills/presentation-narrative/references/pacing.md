# Pacing and Rhythm Guidelines

## Timing Fundamentals

**Speaking rate:** Conversational pace is ~150 words/minute. Presentation pace (with pauses for emphasis, looking at audience, transitioning) is ~130-140 words/minute.

**Per-slide time budget:**

| Talk length | Content slides | Time per slide |
|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| 5 min (lightning) | 4-5 | 60 seconds |
| 10 min | 7-8 | 75 seconds |
| 15 min | 9-11 | 85-95 seconds |
| 20 min | 12-14 | 85-95 seconds |
| 30 min | 18-22 | 80-90 seconds |
| 45 min | 25-30 | 90 seconds |

**Speaker notes word count:** At 135 wpm presentation pace:
- 60-second slide: ~135 words of notes
- 90-second slide: ~200 words of notes
- 120-second slide: ~270 words of notes (this is a long slide — use sparingly)

## One Takeaway Rule

Every slide answers one question: "What's the one thing the audience should remember from this slide?" If the answer requires an "and," the slide is doing too much.

**Test:** Cover the slide content with your hand. Can you state the takeaway in one sentence? If not, split or simplify.

**Exception:** Transitional slides (section dividers) don't need a takeaway — they reorient the audience within the arc.

## Density Spectrum

Slides exist on a density spectrum. A good deck varies density intentionally:

```
SPARSE ◄──────────────────────────────────────────► DENSE
  │                                                    │
  Single quote          Diagram with          Comparison table
  Big statistic         3-5 elements          with 2 columns
  Provocative question  Flowchart             and 4 rows each
  Section title         Timeline
```

**Rule of thumb:** After a dense slide (diagram, comparison), follow with a sparse slide (quote, statistic). This gives the audience mental breathing room.

**Never stack two dense slides back-to-back.** If you have two diagrams that must be adjacent, insert a brief verbal transition slide between them — even just a section title.

## Slide Types and Their Natural Pacing

| Slide type | Density | Natural duration | When to use |
|-----------|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| Quote card | Sparse | 60-90s | Let the quote land, then contextualize |
| Single statistic | Sparse | 45-75s | State the number, let silence do the work |
| Provocative question | Sparse | 60-90s | Pose it, pause, then begin answering |
| Section divider | Sparse | 15-30s | Quick reorientation, don't linger |
| Diagram/flowchart | Medium | 90-120s | Walk through step by step |
| Comparison | Medium-Dense | 90-120s | Guide the eye left-right |
| Timeline | Medium | 75-90s | Hit key dates, skip the rest |
| v-click progressive | Varies | 90-120s | Each click extends the time budget |
| Code sample | Dense | 90-120s | Only for developer audiences |

## Transition Design

Transitions between slides are where amateur talks lose their audience. Every transition should bridge:

**Forward reference (end of current slide):** "So if the manifesto authors never rejected design, what DID they say?"
**Arrival (start of next slide):** [Shows quote from Kent Beck about design]

**Transition patterns:**
- Question bridge: "So what does this mean for us?" → [Answer slide]
- Contrast bridge: "That's what they intended. Here's what happened instead." → [Evidence of corruption]
- Escalation bridge: "And it gets worse." → [More damning evidence]
- Resolution bridge: "But there's a better way to think about this." → [Reframe slide]
- Action bridge: "Which brings us to something concrete." → [CTA slide]

**Write transitions INTO the speaker notes.** The last 1-2 sentences of every slide's notes should set up the next slide.

## Energy Map

A well-paced talk has an energy curve. Map it before building slides:

```
Energy
  │
  │     ╱╲
  │    ╱  ╲      ╱╲
  │   ╱    ╲    ╱  ╲         ╱╲
  │  ╱      ╲  ╱    ╲       ╱  │
  │ ╱        ╲╱      ╲     ╱   │
  │╱                   ╲   ╱    │
  │                     ╲─╱     │
  └──────────────────────────────
   Open  Context  Evidence  CTA
```

- **Open high:** The provocation grabs attention
- **Dip for context:** Necessary but lower energy — keep it short
- **Build through evidence:** Each piece raises the stakes
- **Brief dip before reframe:** Let the evidence settle
- **Peak at CTA:** End with energy and clarity

**If your outline has the energy curve flat or declining, restructure.** A talk that opens with definitions and ends with conclusions is a lecture, not a talk.
